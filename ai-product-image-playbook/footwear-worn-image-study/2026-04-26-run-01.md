# Footwear Worn Image Study — Run 01 (2026-04-26)

Goal: microscopic visual cues + prompt language for **AI-generated ecommerce footwear worn images** (on-foot + product-page closeups). This is not a “trend summary”; it’s a checklist for realism.

Priority scope covered this run:
1) Footwear materials + deformation under weight
2) Bare foot / bare leg micro-details
3) Nude sheer hosiery (10D/15D/20D) + toe constructions
4) Socks by type + tension cues
5) Interaction layers (skin → fabric → shoe contact)

---

## 1) Footwear materials (on-foot) — microscopic cues → prompt phrases

### Smooth leather (uppers)
**Microscopic cues**
- Fine *micro-wrinkle/crease* pattern forms at the vamp flex point (ball of foot) and at the ankle bend; creases are **directional** and anchored to seams/lasting lines (not random).
- Edge paint/piping shows a **clean, slightly rounded** edge; at high magnification, edge paint has a subtle thickness and occasional micro waviness.
- Stitch holes show **punched spacing**; thread has twist + slight fuzz (not laser-perfect).

**Prompt phrases**
- “on-foot product photo, macro crop on vamp flex area, natural leather micro-creasing at the ball line, visible stitch holes with consistent spacing, slightly fuzzy thread texture, realistic edge paint thickness”

**Negative constraints**
- “no smooth plastic sheen, no perfectly flat uncreased leather at flex points, no painted-on stitches, no smeared seam lines”

### Patent leather (uppers)
Patent leather is a **coated/lacquered** leather surface; expect sharp specular highlights and mirror-like reflections. [Wikipedia: Patent leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent_leather)

**Microscopic cues**
- Specular highlight edges are **crisp**; reflections are coherent (not a vague glow).
- Coating can show a faint “orange-peel” micro texture in strong light; dust specks are possible in true macro.

**Prompt phrases**
- “black patent leather pump on foot, high-gloss lacquered finish, crisp white softbox reflections, subtle orange-peel microtexture, clean piping edge, tiny dust specks visible in macro”

**Negative constraints**
- “no matte patent, no milky bloom haze, no muddy reflections, no smeared highlights”

### Suede / nubuck (uppers)
Suede is defined by **nap** (short fibers) with tone shift by direction. [Wikipedia: Suede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suede)

**Microscopic cues**
- Nap direction creates **two-tone shading** with small hand-brushed variations; edges (seams, piping) compress nap and read slightly darker.
- At creases, suede doesn’t form shiny “leather folds”; instead it shows **nap crush** and tonal change.

**Prompt phrases**
- “suede ankle boot on foot, visible nap fibers and directional shading, nap crushed slightly darker along seam edges, tonal shift at flex without glossy creases, macro texture”

**Negative constraints**
- “no smooth painted suede, no shiny leather-like folds, no uniform airbrushed fuzz”

Visual reference (on-foot, suede): [Nordstrom — Steve Madden ‘Obsession’ Bootie](https://www.nordstrom.com/s/obsession-bootie-women/7942966?color=BUFF+SUEDE&size=6.5)

### Knit uppers (engineered knit)
Nike’s Flyknit illustrates knit behavior: looped yarn structure, stretch, and variable knit zones. [Nike: Flyknit](https://www.nike.com/a/flyknit)

**Microscopic cues**
- Knit shows **loop structure** and zone changes (denser around eyelets / toe cap areas, more open elsewhere).
- Tension causes **shape read-through**: toe ridge contours subtly influence the knit surface (especially in tight areas).

**Prompt phrases**
- “macro close-up of knit upper on foot, visible looped yarn structure, engineered zones with varying stitch density, subtle toe contour read-through under tension, realistic yarn fuzz”

**Negative constraints**
- “no smooth neoprene look, no flat printed knit pattern, no melted texture without yarn definition”

### Mesh uppers (engineered mesh)
Engineered mesh should look like a **structured grid** (not ‘screen-door’ blur). Product-page references often call this out explicitly. [Nike Pegasus 41 (engineered mesh upper)](https://www.nike.com/t/pegasus-41-womens-road-running-shoes-RKqt1m/FD2723-001)

**Microscopic cues**
- Mesh has **repeat geometry** (diamond/hex) with controlled irregularity; underlying layers (liner, toe reinforcement) can subtly silhouette.
- Hot-melt overlays or printed reinforcement appear as **thin films** with sharper edges than fabric.

**Prompt phrases**
- “engineered mesh running shoe on foot, visible diamond mesh lattice, underlying sock/liner subtly visible, thin heat-bonded overlay film with crisp edge, macro fabric detail”

**Negative constraints**
- “no heavy blur where mesh should be resolved, no random holes, no coarse netting unless specified”

### Canvas uppers
Canvas is a visible **woven** texture with clear warp/weft; rubber foxing tape often borders it.

Visual reference: [Converse Chuck Taylor All Star Canvas High Top](https://www.converse.com/shop/p/chuck-taylor-all-star-canvas-unisex-high-top-shoe/M9160_090.html?pid=M9160MP&dwvar_M9160MP_color=black&dwvar_M9160MP_size=090&dwvar_M9160MP_width=standard&styleNo=M9160)

**Microscopic cues**
- Warp/weft grid is readable; at seams, canvas compresses and stitch holes distort the weave.
- Rubber foxing/sidewall is matte with micro scuffs; edge line is **clean but not vector-perfect**.

**Prompt phrases**
- “canvas sneaker on foot, crisp woven canvas texture with visible warp/weft, weave distortion near stitch holes, matte rubber foxing tape with tiny scuffs and edge rounding, macro crop”

**Negative constraints**
- “no flat fabric fill, no perfectly uniform weave with no seam distortion”

### Rubber soles / outsoles / outsole edge
**Microscopic cues**
- Rubber reads **matte to satin**, with micro abrasion and dust; traction lugs have sharp-ish edges that round with wear.
- Outsole edge shows a thin line of grime/dust accumulation in real use; the edge is not a perfect CAD silhouette.

Reference: outsole/traction context and terminology (brand-side): [Vibram FAQ](https://ca.vibram.com/on/demandware.store/Sites-vibram_ca-Site/en_CA/LandingPage-FAQ)

**Prompt phrases**
- “macro close-up of outsole edge at toe, matte rubber with micro abrasion, slight dust along edge, traction lug edges slightly rounded, realistic grime in crevices”

**Negative constraints**
- “no glossy plastic sole, no perfectly sharp razor edges, no repeating identical scuff marks”

### Heel counters / rear structure
The heel counter is a structural component; in photos, it should prevent the rear from collapsing. [Wikipedia: Heel counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heel_counter)

**QA cue**
- If a shoe has a heel counter, the back should not look like a soft sock unless the model specifies a collapsible heel.

## 1b) Deformation under weight (on-foot realism)

**Footwear deformation cues**
- Toe box: slight upward toe spring may exist, but when weight is forward you’ll see **flattening** and a subtle “tension line” across the vamp.
- Midsole/outsole: compresses subtly at primary load zones; outsole edge can show a tiny flattening at contact.

**Human contact/pressure cues**
- Plantar load typically concentrates in heel and forefoot; pressure maps are useful mental models for where compression/flattening should occur. [PLOS ONE plantar pressure distribution study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0143197)

**Prompt phrases**
- “standing pose, realistic weight distribution, subtle outsole compression at heel and forefoot, vamp flex creases aligned to ball of foot, no floating shoe”

## 2) Bare foot + bare leg (micro skin realism)

### Skin surface: pores, microrelief, vellus hair
**Microscopic cues**
- Even ‘smooth’ legs show micro texture; pores appear as tiny stippling and microhighlight breakup.
- Vellus hair (“peach fuzz”) can appear as faint, short, directional fibers catching rim light. [Healthline: Peach fuzz](https://www.healthline.com/health/beauty-skin-care/peach-fuzz)

**Prompt phrases**
- “macro leg/ankle crop, visible skin microtexture and pore breakup, subtle vellus hair catching light, natural tone variation with faint blotchiness”

**Negative constraints**
- “no airbrushed skin blur, no porcelain plastic skin, no wax mannequin legs”

### Veins, tendons, bones (anatomical landmarks)
**Microscopic cues**
- Veins: soft blue/green subdermal lines, more visible at ankle/foot dorsum; they should be **diffuse** (not drawn-on).
- Dorsal foot tendons become more prominent with toe extension; extensor digitorum longus is a relevant reference landmark. [Wikipedia: Extensor digitorum longus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensor_digitorum_longus)
- Toe knuckles and MTP region create characteristic folds when toes flex/extend. [Wikipedia: Metatarsophalangeal joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metatarsophalangeal_joint)

**Prompt phrases**
- “top-of-foot close-up, subtle extensor tendons visible under skin with toes slightly extended, faint dorsal veins near ankle, toe knuckle microfolds”

**Negative constraints**
- “no veinless baby-smooth feet, no exaggerated rope tendons, no anatomical misplacement (tendons crossing incorrectly)”

### Contact + compression (shoe opening, straps, and cuffs)
**Microscopic cues**
- Socks/straps can leave transient indentation marks; skin bulges slightly above/below tight bands.
- Sock marks are a common real-life cue for compression and fit. [Medical News Today: sock marks](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sock-marks)

**Prompt phrases**
- “subtle compression line at ankle opening, slight skin indentation and gentle bulge, realistic pressure marks without bruising”

## 3) Hosiery (nude sheer 10D/15D/20D) — show-through rules + toe construction

Key idea: **prompt ‘skin under fabric’ as a layered render**, not as a blur.

### Denier visibility expectations (nude sheer)
Use denier as a proxy for opacity + fiber presence (brand education/guides):
- 10D: very sheer; skin details remain visible; sheen often more noticeable.
- 15D: still sheer; details visible but softened.
- 20D: semi-sheer; stronger smoothing; less vein/tendon visibility; more uniform tone.

Reference guides (conceptual):
- [Magic Hosiery denier guide](https://magichosiery.com/denier/)
- [The Breast Life: what denier means](https://www.thebreastlife.com/what-does-denier-mean-and-how-does-it-affect-your-tights/)

### 10D example: “shadow toe” / almost-invisible reinforcement
Wolford’s “Individual 10” explicitly mentions a shadow-toe concept; use it as a target for on-foot toe realism. [Wolford Individual 10](https://www.wolfordshop.com/en-us/individual-10-tights/18282.html?color=7005)

**Microscopic cues**
- Toe area: reinforcement exists but is subtle; you can still perceive toe outlines.
- Nylon surface: fine, even sheen; highlight should ride over the fabric, not the skin.

**Prompt phrases**
- “nude 10 denier sheer tights, shadow-toe reinforcement (almost invisible), visible toe outlines and knuckle shading beneath fabric, fine nylon sheen, microfilament texture resolved”

**Negative constraints**
- “no painted-on toes, no blur-smoothed foot, no opaque sock-like toe area unless ‘reinforced toe’ specified”

### 15D example (classic sheer)
Falke’s 15D product pages are useful for grounding ‘sheer but present’ fabric. [FALKE Seidenglatt 15](https://www.falke.com/us_en/p/seidenglatt-15-den-women-tights/40015_3009/)

**Prompt phrases**
- “nude 15 denier tights, subtle nylon microtexture, skin pores and veins faintly visible through fabric, toe seam only if specified, controlled satin sheen”

### 20D target (semi-sheer)
**Prompt phrases**
- “nude 20 denier tights, semi-sheer smoothing, reduced vein visibility, toe outlines softened but still readable, matte-to-satin finish, visible fabric layer separate from skin”

### Toe constructions: reinforced toe vs sandal toe vs shadow toe
**Prompt handle**
- If you want open-toe shoes: specify “sandal toe” (no full reinforcement over toes) vs “reinforced toe” (opaque band) vs “shadow toe” (minimal). Definitions vary by brand; always pair with a **visual outcome**.

**Prompt phrases**
- “sandal-toe tights: toe tips and nail beds visible, no opaque reinforcement band; fine nylon layer continues with minimal seam visibility”
- “reinforced toe: slightly more opaque toe cap, clean boundary line, visible toe seam detail”

**Negative constraints**
- “no abrupt hard cutoff band unless reinforced toe requested, no missing toe seams when ‘seamed toe’ requested, no fully transparent fabric with zero fibers”

## 4) Socks (no-show/ankle/crew/dress/terry/wool) — knit anatomy + tension read-through

Reference for sock construction vocabulary: [Darn Tough: how socks are made](https://darntough.com/blogs/the-alternate-stitch/how-socks-are-made)

### Universal sock anatomy cues
**Microscopic cues**
- Toe seam: many socks show a seam ridge or a flatter “linked” seam; it changes how toe outlines read.
- Heel pocket: curvature + knit direction change; thick socks show a clear heel ‘cup’ volume.
- Ribbing: vertical channels with shadowing; ribbing stretches and flattens at high tension.
- Cuff pressure: subtle indentation line and a tiny ‘mushrooming’ above cuff for snug fits.

**Prompt phrases**
- “macro sock close-up, visible ribbing channels, heel pocket shaping, subtle toe seam ridge, knit stitches resolved, light pilling and lint fibers”

**Negative constraints**
- “no seamless painted sock texture, no uniform flat color, no missing heel pocket geometry”

### Thin dress socks (fine gauge)
**Microscopic cues**
- Under tension, toe/knuckle shapes can subtly silhouette through the knit; fabric sheen is minimal to moderate.

**Prompt phrases**
- “fine-gauge dress sock, slightly translucent knit under tension with subtle toe silhouette, clean linked toe seam, minimal fuzz”

### Terry sports socks (cushioned)
**Microscopic cues**
- Terry loops inside; outside can still show plush texture; bulk changes shoe fit and increases pressure at openings.

**Prompt phrases**
- “terry cushioned crew sock, plush loop texture, thicker volume at forefoot and heel, deeper cuff indentation, visible lint fibers”

### Wool / merino socks
**Microscopic cues**
- Fiber looks more irregular; slight halo/fuzz; pilling occurs at high-friction zones.

**Prompt phrases**
- “merino wool sock, visible fiber halo, slight pilling at heel/toe, knit texture resolved, matte finish”

## 5) Interaction layers (skin → fabric → shoe contact) — how to describe it

### Layering template (copy/paste prompt scaffold)
1) **Base skin layer**: tone variation + pores + vellus hair + veins/tendons (as appropriate)
2) **Fabric layer**: denier/thickness + knit/weave geometry + sheen + seam/toe construction
3) **Shoe contact**: opening pressure + strap indentation + vamp compression + outsole contact flattening

**Example (nude 15D + pump)**
- “on-foot macro crop at toe box and ankle opening, natural skin microtexture and faint veins, nude 15 denier tights with subtle nylon microfilament texture and satin sheen, toe outlines softly visible beneath fabric, black patent leather pump with crisp reflections, slight skin compression at shoe opening”

**Example (thin dress sock + loafer)**
- “close-up of loafer vamp on foot, fine-gauge dress sock with resolved knit stitches and linked toe seam, subtle toe silhouette under tension, slight cuff indentation, leather upper with realistic stitch holes and vamp creasing at ball line”

### Failure modes → corrections
- **‘Blur tights’ failure**: fabric becomes a Gaussian blur with no fibers.
  - Fix: add “microfilament/nylon weave resolved, fabric layer separate from skin, no airbrush blur”.
- **‘Sticker toes’ failure**: toes look printed on fabric.
  - Fix: add “toe outlines softened by fabric, highlight belongs to fabric sheen, not skin”.
- **‘Plastic shoe’ failure**: leather looks like smooth vinyl.
  - Fix: add material-specific microtexture (leather grain / suede nap / coating reflections).
- **‘No pressure’ failure**: shoe floats off the foot.
  - Fix: specify “subtle compression at opening, outsole contact flattening, realistic weight distribution”.

## QA checklist (product-page closeups)

At 100% zoom, verify:
- **Material truth**: patent has crisp reflections; suede shows nap + directional tone; knit/mesh shows stitch/lattice (not blur).
- **Seams/stitches**: stitch holes + thread twist; seams follow plausible construction lines.
- **Foot realism**: pores/microtexture present; veins/tendons plausible; toe knuckles and folds make sense.
- **Layer separation**: hosiery sheen sits on fabric; skin detail is visible-but-softened (10D/15D) or more smoothed (20D), never ‘painted’.
- **Pressure/contact**: opening indentation/strap pressure is subtle but present; outsole contact isn’t floating.
